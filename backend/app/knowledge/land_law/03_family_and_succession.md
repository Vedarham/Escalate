# Inherited, family and succession property

## FS-01 | Buying inherited or ancestral property
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: inheritance, ancestral property, legal heirs, heirs consent, succession, family property
sources: Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (ss.6, 8); Indian Succession Act, 1925

When the seller inherited the property, title depends on identifying all legal heirs of the deceased owner under the applicable personal law (Hindu Succession Act, Muslim personal law, Indian Succession Act for Christians and Parsis), and on how the estate was divided. Obtain: the death certificate; a legal-heir or family-tree document; the will and its probate (if any); the partition or release deeds by which other heirs gave up their shares; and updated revenue records. Every legal heir who has not released their share must join the sale. Missing heirs (for example, married daughters or heirs living abroad) are one of the most common causes of later disputes.

## FS-02 | Daughters as coparceners (Hindu joint family property)
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: daughters rights, coparcener, Hindu undivided family, HUF, ancestral, 2005 amendment
sources: Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (s.6, as amended 2005); Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020) 9 SCC 1

Since the 2005 amendment to s.6 of the Hindu Succession Act, daughters are coparceners in a Hindu joint family by birth, with the same rights and liabilities as sons. In Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020) the Supreme Court held that this applies whether or not the father was alive on 9 September 2005. Partitions effected only by a registered deed or court decree before 20 December 2004 are protected. For coparcenary or ancestral land, buyers must therefore check whether daughters' shares exist and obtain their signatures or registered releases. Oral or unregistered "family arrangements" that exclude daughters are risky.

## FS-03 | Legal heir certificate, succession certificate and probate
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: legal heir certificate, succession certificate, probate, letters of administration, will
sources: Indian Succession Act, 1925 (ss.213, 370-372)

A legal heir certificate, issued by revenue authorities in many states, identifies heirs for administrative purposes such as mutation, but it is not conclusive proof of title. A succession certificate from a civil court relates mainly to debts and securities, not land. Probate is a court's certification of a will. Historically, probate has been mandatory for certain wills relating to property in the original jurisdictions of Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai (s.213, Indian Succession Act); this requirement has been the subject of recent legislative change, so check the current position. Elsewhere, buyers and banks often still insist on probate or letters of administration, or on releases from all other heirs, when title flows from a will.

## FS-04 | Minor's share in the property
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: minor, guardian, court permission, minor share, natural guardian
sources: Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956 (s.8)

If any owner or heir is a minor, their share cannot simply be sold by a parent. Under s.8 of the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, a natural guardian needs the prior permission of a court to sell, mortgage or gift a minor's immovable property. A sale without permission is voidable at the minor's instance (the minor can challenge it after becoming an adult, within the limitation period). Ask for the court order permitting the sale and check that the sale price is used for the minor's benefit as the order directs. A guardian may sign on behalf of a minor's undivided interest in joint family property only in limited situations; take legal advice.

## FS-05 | Partition, family settlements and release deeds
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: partition, family settlement, release deed, relinquishment, oral partition
sources: Registration Act, 1908 (s.17); Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation (1976) 3 SCC 119

A partition deed that divides property among family members must be registered and stamped. A family settlement that merely records a partition already made (a memorandum of family arrangement) may not require registration, but such documents are frequently disputed. A release or relinquishment deed, by which an heir gives up their share, must be registered. When buying a portion allotted in a partition, check that all co-sharers signed the partition, that the specific portion is identifiable on the ground and in the revenue records, and that mutation was done according to the partition.

## FS-06 | Will-based title and challenges to wills
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: will, testament, unregistered will, will challenge, executor
sources: Indian Succession Act, 1925 (s.63); Indian Evidence Act / Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023

A will does not need to be registered, but it must be signed by the testator and attested by two witnesses (s.63 Indian Succession Act), and it takes effect only on death. A will can be challenged on grounds of forgery, lack of mental capacity, undue influence or suspicious circumstances, and a later will revokes an earlier one. When the seller's title rests on a will, check: the original will; probate if required or obtained; that the testator actually owned the property and could bequeath it (self-acquired property, or their share only); and releases or no-objections from natural heirs who were excluded.
