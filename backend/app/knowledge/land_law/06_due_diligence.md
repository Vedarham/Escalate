# Due diligence checklists and process

## DD-01 | Master due-diligence checklist for buying land or a plot
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: checklist, documents, due diligence, plot, land purchase, verification
sources: General conveyancing practice; Transfer of Property Act, 1882; Registration Act, 1908

1. Title documents: the mother deed and all registered deeds for at least 30 years, with certified copies from the Sub-Registrar.
2. Revenue records: current record of rights (7/12, RTC or pahani, patta or chitta, khatauni), mutation entries, survey map, and property card or khata.
3. Encumbrance certificate for 13–30 years, plus a CERSAI search.
4. Identity of the sellers and all co-owners and heirs: ID proof, PAN, death and heirship documents.
5. Land use: NA or conversion order, development plan zoning, layout or building approvals.
6. Restrictions: agricultural eligibility, tenure (Class I or II, granted), tribal, trust, acquisition or reservation, forest or CRZ.
7. Litigation search (civil, revenue, high court) and a public notice in newspapers inviting objections.
8. Tax and dues: up-to-date land revenue or property tax and utility bills.
9. Physical survey, boundaries, access and occupants.
10. An advocate's written title-search report before paying more than a token amount.

## DD-02 | Additional checks when buying an apartment or flat
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: apartment, flat, builder, occupancy certificate, OC, commencement certificate, approved plan, society
sources: Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016; state apartment ownership acts; municipal building bye-laws

For a flat, also check:
- the builder's title to the land, or the development agreement and PoA from the landowners;
- the RERA registration;
- the sanctioned building plan, commencement certificate and occupancy or completion certificate (OC or CC), since occupying a building without an OC can lead to penalties, denial of utilities and difficulties with resale;
- that the flat's area and floor match the sanctioned plan;
- the allotment letter and the registered agreement for sale;
- NOCs (fire, environment, airport height where relevant);
- the status of conveyance of the land and building to the society or association;
- for resale, the society NOC, share certificate, original chain of agreements, dues clearance and transfer fees.

## DD-03 | Public notice and objections before purchase
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: public notice, newspaper notice, objections, title claims, due diligence
sources: Conveyancing practice; bank lending norms

Before completing a purchase, buyers (or their advocate) commonly publish a public notice in one English and one local-language newspaper describing the property and the seller, and inviting anyone with a claim (heirs, mortgagees, earlier buyers, tenants) to object within a fixed period, typically 7–15 days. A notice does not create rights, but it surfaces hidden claims cheaply and is evidence of the buyer's good faith and reasonable care (see TT-08). Any objection received must be resolved before paying the balance.

## DD-04 | Registration day and post-registration steps
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: registration process, sub-registrar, e-registration, mutation, after purchase, property tax transfer
sources: Registration Act, 1908; state land revenue codes; municipal acts

On registration day, all sellers and the buyer (or their attorneys) appear before the Sub-Registrar with original ID, PAN, photographs, two witnesses, stamp duty and fee receipts. Biometrics and photographs are recorded and the deed is scanned. Check that the registered deed correctly describes the property, price and payment details. After registration:
- collect the registered deed (or e-registered copy) and the registration extract (for example, Index II in Maharashtra);
- apply for mutation in revenue records and transfer of property-tax and utility accounts;
- file TDS returns where applicable;
- take possession and fence the land;
- keep all originals safe.

Until mutation is complete, keep monitoring the records for fraudulent entries.

## DD-05 | Role of an advocate, surveyor and the bank's legal check
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: lawyer, advocate, title report, surveyor, bank loan, legal opinion, consult
sources: General practice

An independent property advocate should do the title search, review the draft agreement and sale deed, and give a written title report. A licensed surveyor or government measurement confirms the area and boundaries. A bank's legal and technical check, done when a home or plot loan is sanctioned, is a useful extra screen, but it is done for the bank's protection and does not replace the buyer's own due diligence. Consult a lawyer before paying anything substantial, especially for agricultural land, inherited property, PoA sales, properties with tenants or litigation, NRI or minor sellers, and any property whose records do not match.

## DD-06 | Title insurance and indemnity clauses
jurisdiction: all-india
topics: title insurance, indemnity, warranty, sale deed clauses, protection
sources: Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (s.55); general practice

Ask for robust covenants in the sale deed:
- the seller warrants clear title;
- there are no encumbrances, litigation or acquisition;
- all heirs have joined;
- taxes are paid up to the date of sale;
- the seller indemnifies the buyer against loss from any title defect, with a personal undertaking from all sellers.

Title insurance products exist in India but are limited, and an indemnity is only as good as the seller's ability to pay. These protections do not replace proper title investigation.
