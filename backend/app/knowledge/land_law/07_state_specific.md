# State-specific land records and rules

## ST-MH-01 | Maharashtra: 7/12 extract, 8A, ferfar and property card
jurisdiction: maharashtra
topics: 7/12, satbara, 8A, ferfar, mutation entry, property card, mahabhumi, maharashtra land records
sources: Maharashtra Land Revenue Code, 1966; Mahabhumi portal

In rural and peri-urban Maharashtra the record of rights is the 7/12 extract (satbara utara), which shows survey or gat number, area, occupant class (Class I or II), cultivator, encumbrances and remarks such as loans or tenancy. Form 8A shows all holdings of a person in the village. Each change is recorded as a mutation entry (ferfar, Form 6), which should be read to trace how ownership changed. In urban areas covered by a city survey, the property card is the record. Records are available on the state's Mahabhumi portal. Check the "other rights" column of the 7/12 for bank charges, tenants, "new tenure" or restricted-tenure entries, and court orders.

## ST-MH-02 | Maharashtra: registration, Index II and agricultural purchase
jurisdiction: maharashtra
topics: Index II, IGR Maharashtra, ready reckoner, agriculturist certificate, section 63, deemed conveyance, MOFA
sources: Maharashtra Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act, 1948 (s.63); Maharashtra Ownership Flats Act, 1963 (s.11); Maharashtra Stamp Act

Registered transactions in Maharashtra are recorded in Index II, and searches can be made through the IGR Maharashtra portal. Stamp duty is computed on the higher of the price and the ready reckoner value. Agricultural land can generally be bought only by an agriculturist, or with the Collector's permission (s.63 of the Tenancy Act). For flats in housing societies, if the builder fails to convey the land and building to the society, the society can apply for deemed conveyance under s.11 of the Maharashtra Ownership Flats Act (MOFA). Society transfer premiums and NOCs are governed by the society's bye-laws.

## ST-KA-01 | Karnataka: RTC (pahani), khata and EC
jurisdiction: karnataka
topics: RTC, pahani, khata, A khata, B khata, e-khata, bhoomi, Form 15, Form 16, DC conversion
sources: Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964 (s.95); Karnataka Land Reforms Act, 1961; Bhoomi portal

Agricultural land records in Karnataka are the RTC (Record of Rights, Tenancy and Crops, or pahani), available through the Bhoomi system, along with mutation register extracts. Urban properties are recorded in a municipal khata. An "A-khata" is generally issued for properties in approved layouts that pay full tax; a "B-khata" has typically been used for properties in unauthorised layouts or with violations, and may face limits on building approvals and bank loans. The state has been moving to e-khata and has run regularisation schemes, so check the current status. Conversion of agricultural land is a "DC conversion" under s.95 of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act. Encumbrance certificates come in Form 15 and Form 16.

## ST-TN-01 | Tamil Nadu: patta, chitta, adangal, FMB and guideline value
jurisdiction: tamil-nadu
topics: patta, chitta, adangal, FMB sketch, TNREGINET, guideline value, tamil nadu
sources: Tamil Nadu land records (Tamil Nilam); TNREGINET

In Tamil Nadu, the patta is the revenue record naming the person entitled to the land, the chitta records ownership, extent and classification, and the adangal (A-register) records land details and use. The FMB (Field Measurement Book) sketch shows the dimensions and boundaries of the survey field. Buyers should match the patta, chitta and FMB with the sale deeds and the encumbrance certificate, which can be obtained online through TNREGINET, and check the land's classification (for example, "poramboke" indicates government land). Stamp duty is assessed on the guideline value fixed by the government.

## ST-TG-AP-01 | Telangana and Andhra Pradesh: pahani and online land records
jurisdiction: telangana, andhra-pradesh
topics: pahani, adangal, 1-B, meebhoomi, telangana, andhra pradesh, pattadar passbook, land records portal
sources: Telangana and Andhra Pradesh record-of-rights laws and portals

In Andhra Pradesh, the adangal (pahani) and ROR 1-B extracts are available on the MeeBhoomi portal. In Telangana, land records are maintained on the state's integrated land records portal (Bhu Bharati, which replaced Dharani; check the current portal), together with pattadar passbooks. In both states, verify that the pattadar named in the online record matches the seller and the title chain, and check for prohibited-property listings (government, assigned, endowment or Wakf land are listed under s.22-A of the Registration Act as amended by these states), which cannot be registered.

## ST-UP-01 | Uttar Pradesh: khatauni and land use change
jurisdiction: uttar-pradesh
topics: khatauni, khasra, bhulekh, UP revenue code, section 80, uttar pradesh
sources: U.P. Revenue Code, 2006 (s.80); UP Bhulekh portal

In Uttar Pradesh, the khatauni (record of rights) and khasra (field register) are available on the UP Bhulekh portal and show the bhumidhar (holder) and the land's category. Agricultural land must be declared for non-agricultural use under s.80 of the U.P. Revenue Code, 2006 before it is used for construction. Check the land category: gram sabha, government or reserved categories are not saleable by private persons. Also check ceiling limits and whether the seller's rights are transferable.

## ST-DL-01 | Delhi: lal dora, unauthorised colonies and GPA properties
jurisdiction: delhi
topics: lal dora, abadi, unauthorised colony, PM-UDAY, GPA, circle rate, delhi
sources: Suraj Lamp & Industries v. State of Haryana (2012) 1 SCC 656; National Capital Territory of Delhi (Recognition of Property Rights of Residents in Unauthorised Colonies) Act, 2019

Lal dora (abadi) areas in Delhi's villages historically lack formal revenue records, so title is often proved only through possession and unregistered documents, which makes such properties riskier. Many unauthorised colonies were traditionally transacted through GPA, agreement and will chains, which do not convey title (Suraj Lamp). The 2019 Act and the PM-UDAY scheme provide for conferring ownership on residents of eligible unauthorised colonies. Buyers should check whether a conveyance deed or authorisation slip has been issued under that scheme. Stamp duty is computed on circle rates.

## ST-GJ-01 | Gujarat: 7/12, 8A and agricultural restrictions
jurisdiction: gujarat
topics: gujarat, 7/12, 8A, AnyROR, farmer certificate, new tenure, old tenure
sources: Gujarat Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act, 1948 (s.63); Gujarat Land Revenue Code, 1879

Gujarat also uses 7/12 and 8A extracts, available through the AnyROR portal, along with mutation entries (hak patrak). Agricultural land can generally be bought only by a farmer (khatedar), or with permission under s.63 of the Gujarat Tenancy Act. Land of "new and impartible tenure" (new sharat) cannot be transferred without permission and payment of a premium, whereas "old tenure" (juni sharat) land is freely transferable. Check the tenure entry before buying.

## ST-MP-01 | Madhya Pradesh: khasra, B-1 (khatauni), naksha and Bhulekh
jurisdiction: madhya-pradesh
topics: madhya pradesh, MP, khasra, B-1, khatauni, naksha, bhulekh, bhu-abhilekh, bhumiswami, mutation, nazul
sources: Madhya Pradesh Land Revenue Code, 1959; MP Bhulekh (Bhu-Abhilekh) portal

In Madhya Pradesh, the main land records are the khasra (field register showing survey or khasra number, area, holder and land use), the B-1 or khatauni (record of rights listing each holder's khasras), and the naksha (village map). All are available on the state's Bhulekh (Bhu-Abhilekh) portal. Holders with full transferable rights are recorded as bhumiswami under the Land Revenue Code. Match the khasra number, area and holder names in the B-1 with the sale deed and the seller's title chain, and read the mutation (namantaran) orders that show how ownership changed. In towns, check whether the land is nazul (government) land held on lease, since transfer then depends on the lease terms and the lessor's permission.

## ST-MP-02 | Madhya Pradesh: diversion, tribal and allotted land, SAMPADA registration
jurisdiction: madhya-pradesh
topics: madhya pradesh, diversion, section 172, tribal land, section 165, allotted land, patta, SAMPADA, collector guideline, registration
sources: Madhya Pradesh Land Revenue Code, 1959 (ss.165, 172); Registration Act, 1908; MP stamp and registration rules

Using agricultural land in Madhya Pradesh for residential or commercial purposes requires diversion (change of land use) under s.172 of the MP Land Revenue Code. The procedure has been simplified by amendments, so check the current rules and the diversion order and premium paid. Tribal land: under s.165(6), land of a member of an aboriginal tribe (Scheduled Tribe) cannot be transferred to a non-tribal without the Collector's permission, and such transfers are void.

Government-allotted land is a separate restriction from the tribal-land rule. Land allotted by the government to landless persons (government patta or government lessee land) generally cannot be transferred without the Collector's permission either; verify the allotment order and its transfer conditions. Look for "patta", "government lessee" or tribal entries in the B-1 and khasra. Documents are registered electronically through the state's SAMPADA e-registration system, and stamp duty is assessed on the Collector guideline rate.
